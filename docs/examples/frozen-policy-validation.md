For example, a constructed vector of 10,000 observations, with 90 values $-1$, 9,900 zeros and ten values $1$, has mean $-0.008$. With $M=12$ and $\alpha=0.05$, using the rational bound $\log(480)<25/4$ and outward rational rounding gives an empirical-Bernstein upper mean approximately $-0.00155866$, whereas the recorded conservative Hoeffding comparison remains positive. This demonstrates possible non-vacuity under hypothetical bounded independent sampling, not the necessary sample size or expected performance of a future application.
